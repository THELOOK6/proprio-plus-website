Many owners hesitate to change managers because they fear losing rent, records or their tenants’ trust. If you follow a few steps in order, the transition takes a few weeks, with no disruption for tenants.

## 1. Reread your current management contract

Find the length of the mandate, the required termination notice and any fees. The notice period determines when the new manager can take over.

## 2. Choose the transition date

The first day of the month is usually simplest: that month’s rent is collected by a single manager and the accounts close cleanly.

## 3. Notify your current manager in writing

Send the termination notice within the period set out in the contract, and ask for the list of documents you will receive.

## 4. Collect the files

At a minimum, ask for:

- the current leases and any renewal or modification notices sent;
- tenant contact details and payment history;
- any open TAL files;
- service contracts (snow removal, maintenance, insurance) and supplier contacts;
- keys, access codes and remotes;
- the latest financial statements and account balances.

## 5. Inform the tenants

Tenants need to know who to pay rent to, who to contact if there’s a problem, and from what date. A clear written notice prevents payments from going to the wrong place.

## 6. Review after the first month

Check that all rent was collected, supplier accounts are up to date and tenant requests are being handled.
