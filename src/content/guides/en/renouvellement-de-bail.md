In Quebec, a residential lease renews automatically when it ends, on the same terms. To change the rent or another condition, the landlord must send a modification notice within a specific window. A notice given outside that window has no effect on that renewal.

## When to send the notice

| Type of lease | When to give the notice |
| --- | --- |
| Lease of 12 months or more | Between 6 and 3 months before the lease ends |
| Lease of less than 12 months | Between 2 and 1 month before the lease ends |
| Lease with no fixed term | Between 2 and 1 month before the proposed change |

For a 12-month lease ending on June 30, the notice must therefore be given between January 1 and March 31.

## What the notice must include

The notice is in writing, in the language of the lease. It states the change requested (the new rent, or the increase in dollars or as a percentage), the new term of the lease if it changes, and how long the tenant has to respond.

## The tenant’s response

The tenant has one month after receiving the notice to respond. They can accept, refuse the change while staying in the unit, or give notice that they will leave at the end of the lease. A tenant who doesn’t respond is deemed to have accepted.

## If the tenant refuses

If the tenant refuses, the landlord has one month after receiving the refusal to ask the Tribunal administratif du logement to set the rent. Without that application, the lease renews on the same terms.

## How to set the increase

Each year the TAL publishes its tools for calculating a rent increase. Use the official version for the current year, on the Tribunal’s website, to support the amount you request.

This guide summarizes the general rules of the Civil Code of Québec. It does not replace legal advice for your situation.
